Why Y

Every answer costs something.

Electricity. Water. The part where you think. And most of what we ask a machine isn't quite the question we mean, so we ask again. And again.

Y finds the real question first. So you only ask once.

How it works

It asks back.

Y doesn't answer right away. It asks why, and keeps asking, until it's clear what you're really after. Then it answers that. Once.

Why a snail

It started on a fridge in Knoxville.

Avie, 8, noticed grown-ups asking computers a lot of questions, very fast, and wondered what all that speed was costing. The computers. The planet. The part where you think.

So she drew a snail. It carries its home. It never rushes. And it leaves a trail, so you can always see where a question came from.

Company mission statement, complete and unabridged: “Why?”

Under the shell

The shell is a lowercase y.

Every Y carries its name on its back. It was the first thing Avie drew.
